01005 vs 0201: which package should your design use?

7 Nov 20263 min readSMTDFMFine-pitch

01005 measures 0.4 x 0.2 mm and 0201 measures 0.6 x 0.3 mm. The difference is 0.2 mm in each direction — and in production it is the difference between a routine part and one that eats yield. Use the largest package the layout allows; specify 01005 only when board area or signal integrity forces it.

First, a naming trap worth clearing up

Package codes are imperial, in hundredths of an inch. That means "0201" is 0.02 x 0.01 inch, which converts to 0.6 x 0.3 mm. The metric name for the same part is 0603 — which collides with the imperial 0603 (1.6 x 0.8 mm).

So a BOM that says "0603" is ambiguous unless the standard is stated. Ask which one you are getting before the stencil is cut. This single confusion causes more re-quotes than any other line on a BOM.

What actually gets harder at 01005

The placement machine is rarely the limit. Four other things are:

  • Paste volume. The stencil aperture becomes small enough that the aperture's area-to-wall-area ratio decides whether paste releases at all. This pushes toward thinner stencils and finer paste classification.
  • Tombstoning and skew. Less solder, less self-alignment force during reflow. The part has to land right rather than being pulled right.
  • Handling. Parts this size are difficult to rework by hand, so a single defect is often a scrap decision rather than a repair.
  • Inspection. Optical inspection needs enough contrast and resolution to judge a fillet measured in tens of microns.

None of these are reasons to avoid 01005. They are reasons to pay for them only when the design earns it.

When is 0201 the better answer?

Almost always, unless one of these is true:

  1. The board is area-constrained — a module, a wearable, an implant, where the enclosure is the requirement.
  2. Loop inductance matters — decoupling placed closer to the pin, which is a real electrical reason, not a cosmetic one.
  3. The assembly is already dense — if you are at 0.3 mm IC pitch, the passives are no longer the hardest thing on the board.

If none apply, moving from 0201 to 0402 usually lowers cost and defect rate without changing function. That is a design decision worth making explicitly rather than inheriting from a reference design.

What to confirm with the manufacturer before you commit

  • Can they place it, and on which line? A capability claim without a line assignment is not a commitment.
  • What paste and stencil combination do they use for it?
  • What is their first-article process for a package this size — is it inspected optically, by X-ray, or both?
