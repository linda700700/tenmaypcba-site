What PCBA costs, and how to read a quote that holds

Six notes on the arithmetic behind a PCBA price: which costs are fixed per job, how setup divides across a batch, where consigning flips, and why a second order costs less than the first. Written for the buyer who has a quote in hand and cannot tell what is inside it.

6 notes23 min end to endLast reviewed 17 Nov 2026

In 60 seconds

  1. 01Two quotes for the same board are rarely the same offer. The difference is what each one leaves unsaid: test, yield, material ownership, and what happens when a part is short.
  2. 02Fixed cost divided by quantity is most of the small-batch answer. Changeover, kitting and material minimums are the rest.
  3. 03Consigning is cheaper only when you already own the parts. The flip point is driven by purchasing labour and shortage risk, not by the assembly unit price.
  4. 04A reorder is cheaper because the fixed work is already done — and only while four things stay unchanged.
  5. 05On a board everyone can quote, the price is the only visible variable, which is exactly why the invisible process steps decide who is cheaper in year two.
